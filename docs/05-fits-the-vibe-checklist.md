# Lesson 5 — The "Fits the Vibe?" Checklist

A cover can be *technically* correct — right bleed, right spine,
letter-perfect title — and still fail, because it feels off. "Off"
compared to what? The shelf. This lesson is a checklist you run
against the covers that already sell (and the covers of your direct
rivals) to catch that wrongness early.

## The 60-Second Thumbnail Test

Do each, in order, without hovering:

1. **Export at 100 px tall** (or just zoom far out) — pretend you
   shrank your cover into an Amazon search result.
2. Look for **20 seconds total**, and answer:
   - What genre is this? (Name it out loud. Did it match?)
   - Where did my eye land first? (Was that what I wanted?)
   - Can I read the title letters at all? (Some covers are
     wordless by design — that's a legitimate, rare choice.)
   - Does it look like a book, a poster for a movie, a flyer for an
     event? Book wins.

## The Squint Test

Look at the same thumbnail through squinted eyes. Detail evaporates;
what is left is: shapes, contrast, color temperature. Ask:

- Are there **three clean chunks** (e.g., title / art / author) or
  ten fuzzy ones?
- Is there one bright accent pulling the eye? Is it *pointed at
  something meaningful* (the title, a face, the intriguing object)
  or is it an accident?

## The Shelf Test

Open Amazon, click into your genre's bestseller list, arrange 12
screenshot-pulled bestseller thumbs in a row (a "context board" — you
may do this digitally or with paper printouts) and put yours in the
middle. Ask:

- [ ] Cover-wise, is mine **recognizably from that lineup**?
- [ ] Is mine visibly distinct — different enough not to read as a
      knock-off of one specific rival title's poor cover?
- [ ] Does its palette fit the shelf's *energy band*? (Brightened
      hues still fit if the *value* fits — the light-dark rhythm.)
- [ ] Title font's weight and mood match the shelf (not gothic-metal
      heaviness where the shelf leans light and airy)?

## The Flip-Test / Fits-Me Test

Finally, the human one:

- [ ] If a friend who reads this genre saw only the cover, would
      they at least name the genre correctly?
- [ ] Is there *one* oddity I'm keeping only because I made it?
      (Designer bias is real; list candidate cuts and keep the courage.)
- [ ] Tone check: is the title font's mood the same as the tagline's
      and the art's? (Say the style words out loud: "eerie," "cosy,"
      "clinical" — is the whole cover telling one story?)

## The Two-Column Scorecard

Copy this onto a sticky note, score each 1–5, and be brutally honest:

| Question | 1–5 |
|---|---|
| Genre is readable in 2 seconds |  |
| Title readable at 100 px height |  |
| Exactly one dominant visual anchor |  |
| 2 fonts or fewer, hierarchy clear |  |
| Palette feels native to the shelf |  |
| Readable as "a book cover" (not a poster/flyer) |  |

**Scoring:**
- 24–30 : ready for the amateur-tells sweep (Lesson 7), then export.
- 18–23 : strong bones; fix the two lowest scores between this and an
          hour's honest work, then re-run.
- Below 18 : do not fix piecemeal; go back to Lesson 1 with the shelf
  screenshot tests and restart the composition.

## What "distinct" turns out to mean

Beginners overthink distinctness; experienced designers under-think
shelf *cohesion*. Your cover should make a specific trio of moves:

1. Similar-enough to the shelf that genre registers instantly
2. A clear twist — palette temperature flip, unusual main-image
   placement, a quieter texture, one bold color
3. Zero accidental resemblance to one identifiable rival cover
