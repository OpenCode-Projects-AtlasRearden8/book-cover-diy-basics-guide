Lesson 0 — Start Here

Who this manual is for

You have written a book. You want a cover that looks like a real published book, you do not want to hire a designer, and you do not want to spend money you do not have. This manual is for you.

Written by Asdale Dynamics, LLC in the same plain-English, step-by-step spirit as The Massive AI Manual: like a friend walking you through it, never condescending.

What this manual is: a beginner's guide to designing clean, KDP-ready (Kindle Direct Publishing) print and ebook covers yourself, using free tools — Canva (free tier), GIMP, or an Affinity trial.

What this manual is not: it is not legal advice, not a substitute for the official KDP help pages (specs change — always confirm against KDP's own cover calculator before you upload), and not a validation tool. If you want software that checks a file against KDP math, that is a different project (the kdp-cover-bleed-checker-cli tool). This is the learning manual; that is the checking tool.

The three big ideas before you touch a tool

  1. A cover is a poster, not a painting. It has one job: tell a browsing reader the genre and mood in under three seconds, at the size of a postage stamp. Everything else — your beautiful art, your clever symbolism — must survive being shrunk to a thumbnail.
  2. Print has unforgiving math. Ebook covers are just big JPGs. Print covers have margins, bleed, and a spine whose width depends on how many pages your book has. Get the math wrong and KDP bounces the file or the printer crops your title off the edge.
  3. The vibe must be earned. A thriller cover that looks like a cookbook, or a romance that looks like a legal thriller, confuses readers. We will spend a lesson on genre expectations and a whole checklist on the "does this feel right?" test.

Choosing your tool

All three free routes can produce a clean cover. Pick one and stick with it for your first cover:

  • Canva (free tier) — fastest, friendliest, runs in a browser. Weakness: fine control (exact 0.125" bleed) requires care with custom-size designs, and some pro assets are locked. Lesson 6A.
  • GIMP — free and powerful, closer to Photoshop. Weakness: the interface takes a weekend to befriend. Lesson 6B.
  • Affinity trial — a short free trial of professional software. Weakness: the trial clock is ticking; plan your weekend around it. Lesson 6C.

What you need before starting

  • Your book's trim size (e.g., 6" x 9") — Lesson 2.
  • Your page count from your formatted interior file — needed for spine width (Lesson 3).
  • Your title and author name, final spelling.
  • The paper type you will use (white, cream, or color premium — chosen when you set up the paperback in KDP).

A word on "screenshots-style spot checks"

We do not publish anything from this manual, and nothing here connects to any publishing platform. Where we talk about KDP, we describe spot checks: things you look for in your exported file — like flipping through the KDP previewer on screen — the way you would eyeball a screenshot. Save your "back cover," "front cover," and "spine strip" as images on your computer and check those images against the checklists in Lessons 7 and 8.
