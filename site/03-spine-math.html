Lesson 3 — Spine Math for Print

The spine is the one part of the cover where being wrong is visible from across a room: a spine too narrow for your letters, or front/back art that got baked into the spine area because you guessed the width. Here is the actual math, and it is friendlier than it sounds.

The spine formula (with honest caveats)

Spine width = pages x multiplier, where the multiplier depends on your paper stock. The multipliers below are the widely-cited KDP industry approximations for common stocks:

Paper stock Multiplier (inches per page)
Black & white interior on white paper 0.002252
Black & white interior on cream paper 0.0025
Standard color interior 0.002347
Premium color interior 0.002347 (use KDP's template for exact)

Example: 320-page novel, white paper b&w:

spine = 320 x 0.002252 = 0.72064"  → build at ~0.72"

The friendly warning. These multipliers are approximations that drift slightly with press settings. The always-safe workflow is: use the KDP cover-dimension calculator (free, on kdp.amazon.com) to generate an exact-size template PNG or PSD, then design inside that template. This lesson teaches you the math so the calculator's numbers never surprise you.

What changes with the size of the spine

  • Below ~100 pages: the spine is thin (a 90-page 6x9 on white paper is about 0.20"). Do not put text on the spine. It will likely be illegible and may not survive the folding/gluing process cleanly. Keep the spine area artwork-only, matching front and back.
  • 100+ pages: spine text is acceptable. Use a font sized so the title reads clearly, vertically centered, with breathing room equal to at least ~0.0625" above and below the text block.
  • Horizontal centering: spine letters should be optically centered (slightly-high of true center feels right; nobody knows why; trust the eye).

A worked example you can copy

Book: 6" x 9" trim, 288 pages, b&w on cream paper.

spine  = 288 x 0.0025 = 0.72"
wrap width  = (6 x 2) + 0.72 + 0.25 = 12.97"
wrap height = 9 + 0.25              = 9.25"
front panel starts at: 0.125 (bleed) + 6 + 0.72 = 6.845" from left
back panel ends at:    0.125 (bleed) + 6        = 6.125"  from left
spine occupies: 6.125" to 6.845"

Design your wrap at 12.97" x 9.25" @ 300 DPI (3891 x 2775 px) and use vertical guides at 6.125" and 6.845" so the spine has its own lane.

DPI math (why 300)

KDP requires print covers at 300 DPI. Convert inches to pixels simply by multiplying by 300:

12.97" x 9.25"  →  (12.97 x 300) x (9.25 x 300)  px
                →  3891 x 2775 px

Same trick for ebook (which is measured in pixels directly — Lesson 2).

Three spine mistakes

  1. Eyeballing the width. "Looks about an inch" is how people end up with a 0.72" spine on a 1.06" book. Compute; never guess.
  2. Backing spine art with way too much contrast between spine and panels, so any tiny press shift makes the spine look like a sticker. Keep the cover background continuous across the spine when you can.
  3. Spine text touching the edges of the spine lane. Give the text block vertical padding inside the spine lane.

Cheat sheet: quick spine widths

Assuming white paper, b&w interior (0.002252 in/page):

Pages Spine width (in) Spine text?
90 0.20 No
120 0.27 Borderline; lean no
180 0.41 Yes, small type
240 0.54 Yes
320 0.72 Yes, comfortable
448 1.01 Yes, plenty of room

Cream paper (0.0025): same page counts give 0.23 / 0.30 / 0.45 / 0.60 / 0.80 / 1.12 respectively — cream is always a touch thicker.
