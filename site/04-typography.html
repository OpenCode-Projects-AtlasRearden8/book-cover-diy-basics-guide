Lesson 4 — Typography That Doesn't Fight You

Ninety percent of "amateur-looking" covers fail at the type before the art gets a chance to be judged. This lesson is not about taste; it is about five mechanical habits that make text look professionally set even on a first cover.

Habit 1 — Use two fonts, at most

A good default system:

  • Title font: a display font with personality, chosen because of its genre vibes (Lesson 1's table).
  • Author/series/other font: a clean "worker" font — neutral serif or sans — that stays out of the way.

If you add a third font, you had better have 50 covers of experience and a reason. Two covers into your journey? Two fonts.

Habit 2 — Build hierarchy before you decorate

Typography hierarchy = which line your eye reads first, second, third. Decide, in this order:

  1. Title — biggest, the most dominant invitation
  2. Author name (if you are a household name; if not, large enough to read at thumbnail)
  3. Series / tagline / all other text: smallest, quietest

Never let a tagline compete with a title.

Habit 3 — Contrast, contrast, contrast

Rules that win more games than taste does:

  • Light text on dark, dark text on light. Never light-on-light ("cream on beige") or dark-on-dark ("navy on maroon"). Test: take a screenshot, squint. Can you still read the title? Good.
  • One color shift per element, not a rainbow per letter.
  • Outlines and shadows are medicine, not seasoning. Use them only when a title must cross from dark to light background — and then keep them thin (1–2 px at cover scale) and a single color.

Habit 4 — Letter spacing is your free upgrade

Tracking (the space between letters) is the difference between "typed" and "typeset." Rules you can trust:

  • ALL-CAPS words need extra tracking. "TITLE" with normal default spacing looks cramped; "T I T L E" (lightly tracked) looks designed.
  • Lowercase sentences need nearly default-to-slightly-negative.
  • Thin display fonts look better tracked out a little; fat display fonts, tracked in a little.

In Canva use "Spacing"; in GIMP/Affinity, the text tool's letter spacing field. Experiment on one word, six ways; pick the calmer one.

Habit 5 — Alignment has a system

  • Left-aligned title, left-aligned author: editorial and modern.
  • Centered title, centered author: prestige and traditional.
  • Never one centered line and one stray left-aligned line unless it is deliberate. If unsure, run a mini test: each line's left edge and its center should land comfortably inside the rhythm of the other lines.

Pick left or center, then be faithful to it.

Free fonts worth starting from

All free and open-source (serve them up from Google Fonts in Canva, or download OTFs from foundries / use the marked "free" license):

  • Display / title: Cinzel (epic/historical), Bebas Neue (thriller/urban), Playfair Display (romance/prestige), Cinzel Decorative (fantasy), Oswald (crime/noir), Orbitron (sci-fi), Creepster (horror — use carefully), Lora (warm/memoir).
  • Worker / author: Lato, Montserrat, Open Sans, Source Serif, Crimson, Merriweather.

Check the license before you use any font downloaded outside Google Fonts, and prefer open-source fonts so your commercial use is simple.

Three typography mistakes that scream "first cover"

  1. Default settings everywhere — default tracking, default line height, default everything. Defaults are a starting point, not a style.
  2. Stacking three "decor" tricks on one word — outline + shadow + gradient + texture + glow. Pick one, not all five.
  3. Title edge-to-edge. A title that touches the cover's side margins looks like a printing error. Give the title breathing room on both left and right (See Lesson 2's safe margin zone).

The 30-second type audit

Before export, zoom out to 100 px height:

  1. Is the title readable at that size?
  2. Are there exactly two fonts (or fewer)?
  3. Is there ONE visual anchor of the type, not three?
  4. Is the all-caps text tracked properly?
  5. Squint — does the author name disappear? (If you are a new/unknown author, that's fine — but decide and know.)
